A transit office has drafted a passenger notice about road works. The route is usually a loop, with buses leaving Central Square, calling at School Road and Mill Bridge, and then returning through Market Street. During the works the Market Street stop will be closed, but Central Square, School Road, and Mill Bridge remain open. The temporary route will return along Riverside Avenue. There is a temporary stop called Riverside East, 250 metres from the closed stop. It is on a level path with a pedestrian crossing, but the crossing is controlled only by traffic lights, not by staff.

The diversion starts on Monday 16 November at the beginning of service and is expected to finish on Friday 27 November after the last bus. The route number stays 14. Weekday departures from Central Square remain every 20 minutes from 06:00 until 20:00. On Saturdays buses run every 30 minutes from 07:00 until 19:00; on Sundays every hour from 09:00 until 18:00. The closure itself spans two weekends. The diversion is expected to add five minutes to each loop, and the office has added a spare bus so that the departure interval can stay unchanged.

The old notice reads: 'Because of necessary infrastructure improvement works, some passengers may possibly experience alterations to their normal boarding arrangements. Please be advised that the affected stop will be replaced by an alternative facility for a temporary period. Services should remain broadly similar, although delays cannot be excluded. Customers are kindly requested to familiarise themselves with the appropriate changes and plan their travel accordingly.' It does not name the closed stop, dates, route, or replacement location. A staff member wants to add 'the route will not operate normally', but this may leave readers unsure whether buses are running at all.

The office can print one A4 notice and display the same wording on its website. A map will appear beside the text, but the text must still be useful if the map is not visible. Telephone assistance is available at 555 0148 from 08:00 to 16:00 on weekdays. Those are assistance hours, not bus operating hours. The notice should not promise that every journey will take exactly five extra minutes, because traffic conditions vary. Drivers will announce the change on the first two days, and stops will display the notice one week before the diversion begins.

Two facts are still uncertain: contractors have not confirmed whether the work may finish early, and the office has not received a final accessibility inspection for the temporary stop. It should state only the known level-path access and avoid calling the whole temporary stop 'fully accessible'. If the work runs late, the office will publish an update at the normal route-information page. Do not invent a web address or a new contact channel.

Rewrite the passenger notice in clear language. Include dates, the closed and replacement stops, continuing service, and where to get help. Keep it short enough for quick reading and preserve the uncertainty about the finish date.
