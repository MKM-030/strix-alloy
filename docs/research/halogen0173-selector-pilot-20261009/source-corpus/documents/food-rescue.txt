A volunteer group collects unsold food from four bakeries and delivers it to a community kitchen. The arrangement is eight months old. Volunteers use their own cars, and each bakery places food in labelled containers before closing. The kitchen checks the labels and sorts the food on arrival. The group is considering a fifth bakery, but it has no spare refrigerated vehicle. The current collections are bread and unfilled pastries, which the kitchen's existing policy accepts. The new bakery also wants to donate cream-filled cakes.

The current route takes between 65 and 85 minutes, depending on closing times and traffic. The first bakery closes at 17:30, the second at 18:00, the third at 18:15, and the fourth at 18:30. The kitchen accepts deliveries until 19:15. Last month the group made 24 routes, delivered 486 kilograms, and arrived late three times. Two late arrivals followed a road closure; the third followed a missing label that a volunteer had to resolve. These are recorded explanations from route notes, not a complete causal study.

The proposed fifth bakery is twenty minutes beyond the fourth. It closes at 18:45 and estimates donations of eight to twelve kilograms per collection. Adding it to the existing route would require returning toward the kitchen afterward, another 25-minute journey. A volunteer proposed collecting from that bakery first, but it will not prepare its donation earlier than 18:35. Another proposed leaving the existing fourth bakery for a second car. Only two of the twelve volunteers can reliably drive on weekdays, and one has said they cannot add another evening each week.

The kitchen manager supports receiving more bread but has asked the group to verify handling requirements before accepting the filled cakes. The bakery says the cakes will be packed cold, but has not specified the acceptable transport temperature or maximum duration. The manager has not approved a new food category. The group should not describe the cakes as safe solely because they feel cold at collection. It can discuss operational conditions and refer the unresolved handling question back to the kitchen's established process.

The group has a small grant of 900 euros. It could buy insulated containers or reimburse a limited number of taxi deliveries, but nobody has priced those options. The grant cannot pay for a vehicle. Last month 82 percent of delivered food was served or frozen within a day. The rest was unsuitable or exceeded the kitchen's capacity. That figure shows that added kilograms do not automatically become additional meals. The kitchen needs a short trial that records arrival time, accepted quantity, and discarded quantity separately.

Write a recommendation about including the fifth bakery. Address route feasibility, volunteer capacity, and the unresolved product-handling question. Suggest a bounded trial and one criterion for continuing it. Do not assume new funding or additional volunteers will appear.
