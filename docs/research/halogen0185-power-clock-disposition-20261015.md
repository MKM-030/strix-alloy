The historical telemetry shows a real operating-range difference, not its cause. In the earlier measured MTP sensor cohort the GPU mean was 2677.17 MHz / reported 135.58 W / 79.54°C; the conditioned cohort was 1634.46 MHz / reported 54.36 W / 55.79°C. Their recorded decode means were 46.355 and 41.625 tok/s. These sequential 0.16.2 observations are not a matched clock intervention or sustained 0.18.5 baseline. One-hertz queries mix prefill and generation, sensor refresh is unknown, and `GPUPower` is not qualified package/board power. They establish neither a temperature limit nor a power cap, and the frequency ratio must not be converted into a predicted speedup. [Conditioned comparison](halogen-conditioned-control-20261005.md).

A useful clock intervention would require a supported adjustable range, known current/default value and restoration, plus a bottleneck that responds to that clock. Even a hypothetical GPU-frequency ratio r only changes complete time by `T'/T = (1-f) + f/r` when f is the measured frequency-sensitive fraction; neither f nor an admissible r is established here. Extra package power, thermal settling and shared CPU/memory contention are unpriced. There is therefore no defensible bounded return or clock/power candidate to run alongside the finite PLD comparison.

The earlier source audit also closes global alternatives: the experimental AMD SMI WSL clock/performance setters are unsupported through its WSL dispatch; WDDM engine/memory maxima are getters rather than tuning controls; the existing ROCr power-optimization escape is a lifecycle call with unverified effective semantics, not a new supported knob; Ryzen Master platform documentation did not qualify FP11; and no BOSGAME BeyondMax/BIOS 3.10 software power/fabric contract was found. These are retained source findings, not a new release search.
